Secondary Aromas and Flavours

Aromas and flavours of post-fermentation winemaking

Yeast (lees, autolysis, flor)biscuit, pastry, bread, toasted bread, bread dough, cheese, yogurt
Malolactic conversionbutter, cheese, cream
Oakvanilla, cloves, coconut, cedar, charred wood, smoke, chocolate, coffee
LeesDead yeast cells and grape solids that settle at the bottom of the tank, barrel, or bottle after fermentation. Contact adds texture and flavor.
AutolysisDead yeast’s own enzymes breaking the cells down over months to years. Releases compounds that add brioche, biscuit, and nutty notes and a creamy texture. Most important in traditional-method sparkling wines.
FlorA film of living yeast that grows on the surface of wine in a partly filled barrel. It’s found in Fino and Manzanilla sherry and in Jura’s vin jaune. It protects the wine from oxygen and adds saline, almond, and bready notes.

Umami can be difficult to isolate because umami tends to be present with other tastes such as salt that balance the negative effects of umami.

Examples of foods with umami taste are:

  • Cooked or dried mushrooms.
  • Air-dried meats.
  • Aged cheeses.
  • Miso.

It is usually desirable for the flavour intensities of the food and wine to be matched.
